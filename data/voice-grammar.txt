# What the driver can say and have recognised exactly.
#
# One phrase per line. Blank lines and lines starting with '#' are ignored.
#
# This exists because free dictation does badly on precisely the utterances
# that matter here. Measured on the rig on 2026-08-05: a long sentence came
# back at 44% confidence and roughly right, while "hate all factory" and
# "her as" - both short phrases - came back at 56% and 36% and were nonsense.
# Dictation leans on sentence context and a three-word phrase gives it none,
# but a three-word phrase under load is exactly what gets said while driving.
#
# A rule grammar searches only these lines instead of the whole language, so a
# match is near-certain even with a helmet on and an engine running. Dictation
# still runs underneath for anything not listed, tagged as low confidence.
#
# Read at startup and copied beside the exe by CMake, so the vocabulary can be
# changed here without rebuilding or reinstalling anything on the rig - the
# same arrangement as corner-names.txt.

# Marking a moment. The most valuable thing he can say: it timestamps where he
# felt something wrong, so the telemetry can be read at that exact point.
mark
mark that

# What the car did. These map onto the two failure modes already in the model.
understeer
oversteer
i lost the front
i lost the rear
the front pushed
the rear stepped out

# Whether the last change helped. Confirming a fix held is half the method.
that was better
that was worse
that felt good
that felt bad
no difference

# Traffic and mistakes, so a bad lap can be discounted rather than analysed.
traffic
my mistake
i went off
ignore that lap

# Talking to the coach.
say again
repeat that
stop coaching
start coaching
quiet please
what was that lap
