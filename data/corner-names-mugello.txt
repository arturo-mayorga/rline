#
# THESE ARE TRACK TURN NUMBERS, NOT DETECTOR INDICES. The driver asked for
# numbers on 2026-08-11 - "I can't keep track of corner names" - and the only
# numbers that mean anything to him are the ones on the circuit map. The
# detector's own 1..6 must never be spoken: its corner 5 is turn twelve, and an
# entire session was once coached on detector numbering, sending him to the
# wrong piece of road every single time.
#
# So each line below is the turn number where that detected corner BEGINS -
# specifically its turn-in, because that is where the braking cue applies.
#
# The detector finds SIX corners across fifteen numbered turns here - far
# coarser than Road America's eleven of sixteen, because most of Mugello is
# fast enough that the detector never sees a corner at all. Every entry below
# therefore stands for a stretch of road, not a single turn. What each one
# swallows is written next to it, and the number spoken is the FIRST turn of
# that stretch - so "turn ten" can mean a fault that is really at turn eleven.
# Where a fault is known to sit at the far end of a span, say the exact turn
# from the relay rather than relying on this file.
#
# Anchors, both ends of the range:
#   detected 1 is the only corner braked at 0.95 - turn one, the slowest
#   detected 3 is the only corner with essentially no brake (0.13) - and it
#     covers the whole flat-out block from turn six to turn nine
#
# Written as words, not digits: SAPI reads "10" in this context as "ten" but
# reads a bare digit inconsistently between voices, and a corner number heard
# wrong is the one error this file exists to prevent.
